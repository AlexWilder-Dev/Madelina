# Madelina — research notes

Compiled 17 October 2026 for a speculative redesign pitch. Every fact below has a source; anything
not confirmed is marked **unconfirmed**. Raw page text is in `research/raw/*.txt`, original images in
`research/img/`.

## Who they are

- Family-run by **Eugen and Mady**, who moved from Romania to London, spent almost a year working in a
  local coffee shop, then opened **East Dulwich in October 2013** and **Brixton in 2016**. "We are
  planning to open more London branches in the future." (Volcano Coffee Works "Hot Spots" interview,
  28 March 2024 — volcanocoffeeworks.com/blogs/journal/volcano-hotspots-blog-madelina)
- Both branches used to trade as **Piece of Cake Cafe**: their own site's phone links still point to
  Google searches for "piece of cake cafe brixton" / "piece of cake dulwich", and Restaurant Guru's URLs
  for both are `Piece-Of-Cake-Cafe-London(-2)`. The Squarespace site (and so the Madelina name online)
  dates from October 2021.
- Coffee: **Volcano Coffee Works**, "just a 5 min walk from our Brixton HQ". Interview says both
  branches serve **The Brixton** blend; Madelina was "one of our first customers to serve the Delta
  Blend", and Delta Blend bags are on their retail shelf in the photo. Favourite drink to make: "an Oat
  Flat White … We cater for a lot of plant-based customers."
- Instagram/Facebook `@madelinalondon` (~7.5k followers, ~1,050 posts per search snippet). Bio (search
  snippets, wording unconfirmed): roughly "Coffee | Brunch | Wine | Small Plates · Brixton & Dulwich ·
  serving 7 days a week · DM to book wine nights". Older handles still linked from listings:
  instagram.com/madelinabrixton, facebook.com/PieceOfCakeBrixton.
- **Instagram, Facebook, Threads and TikTok could not be read** (429s and login walls; mirrors behind
  bot checks). No post captions were available, so their social voice is inferred only from their
  website copy.

## The rooms

**Brixton, 105 Acre Lane (corner).** Photo `IMG-6839.jpg` (on their home page): a corner Victorian
shopfront painted black, gold **M monogram** with leaf sprig on the fascia, black retractable awnings,
folding slatted bistro chairs and tables on the wide corner pavement, hanging baskets of pink flowers,
a chalk A-board ("Coffee Time / Tea"), a **Progress Pride flag** over the door. Listings tag it LGBTQ+
friendly, dog-friendly, gender-neutral toilets, wheelchair-accessible entrance (Google attributes via
hampsteadvillagelondon.com). Inside (Volcano photos, March 2024, most likely Brixton): **sage-green
walls**, **brass dome pendants** with warm bulbs, wood counter, glass cake case, chalk menu boards,
reclaimed-plank shelves on brick holding Volcano coffee bags and **painted pottery for sale** ("Handmade
ceramics", "Tea pot £40", "Large jug £34"); dineawardslondon also says the coffees come "in unique
ceramic cups available for purchase". Reviewers mention a "charming rear outdoor area" and a "cozy
terrace" (Restaurant Guru / dineawards summaries). Small indoor space (spinach.guide).

**Dulwich, 75 East Dulwich Grove.** Photo `IMG-6260.jpg` (their Dulwich menu page): flat frontage,
black fascia with gold **MADELINA** and "Alfresco Dining", **"No. 75"** painted top-left, grey scalloped
awning, black metro tiles under the window, four bistro tables outside, chalk A-board listing brunch
dishes. Homegirl London (Aug 2025): "From the outside, it’s pretty unassuming, but inside, they’ve
created a warm and characterful space. It’s calm and cosy"; "a piano in the corner, leafy plants,
armchairs, and shelves filled with books, vases and trinkets. It feels more like someone’s front
parlour than a café". **Back garden**: "a quiet little decked area at the back with a sun sail and a
privacy wall"; ma.to: "scenic garden terrace perfect for alfresco dining throughout the summer
months". Five minutes' walk from East Dulwich station, close to Lordship Lane.

**The plates.** Every food photo is on the same painted earthenware: terracotta body, cream glaze,
rims of ochre "eyes" with red centres between thin green/blue/brown trails, some with cobalt or deep
green grounds; matching drip-pattern cups. Dishes are finished with **edible flowers** (pansies,
violas, calendula), pomegranate and micro-herbs. Photos are shot overhead on slatted wooden tables
outdoors, with marigold-coloured flowers and teal-green napkins. This is the strongest visual
identity they have, and it is entirely theirs.

## Who comes when

- **Weekday mornings (8am–3pm both)**: locals before work, laptops, "a solo brunch or a quiet catch-up"
  (Homegirl); "grabbing breakfast before work or settling in with a paper on a Saturday" (Urbanary).
- **Weekend brunch**: the busy time; "Booking is recommended, especially at weekends" (Kid Friendly
  London, Dulwich); kids menu, high chairs and buggy access at Dulwich.
- **Friday & Saturday evenings, Brixton, 6–11pm: wine nights** — "recently opened wine nights every
  Friday and Saturday" with "cured meats and fine cheese boards" (TripAdvisor reviewer, c. Dec 2024,
  search snippet only). Booking by Instagram DM.
- **Summer**: the Dulwich garden; "DM to book … for visiting the Dulwich garden" (ma.to FAQ);
  "Lovely little outdoor garden too" (Romi S, Venuescanner, Sep 2025).
- **Brixton's crowd**: "Cafe Madelina can be spotted from right down the street… often contrasted by
  Pride flags flapping in the wind and crowds of London's hip youngsters taking up the tables outside"
  (LondonxLondon, Nov 2023). Thatsup's Brixton café guide (Jul 2025): "Homely Brixton café Madelina is a
  popular spot for brunch… all served on colourful crockery."
- **Dulwich's crowd**: families (kids menu, high chairs, buggy access — Kid Friendly London), solo
  brunchers and quiet catch-ups (Homegirl). Used as a door-knocking meeting point by the local Green
  Party in April 2026 (search snippet), i.e. a known local landmark.

1. Phone numbers are links to Google searches for the old name (Piece of Cake) instead of `tel:`.
2. No opening hours anywhere, and nothing about the Friday/Saturday evenings.
3. No booking, wine-night, garden or hire information; everything goes through Instagram DMs.
4. Menus have no coffee, tea, smoothies, cakes, pastries, wine or prices for drinks.
5. A stray empty shopping cart (/cart) in the header.
6. The link preview is the logo on transparent (renders as gold on white/black), title "Cafe Madelina |
   Discover Vibrant Brunch — Visit Today".
7. The best asset — the food on painted plates — is shown as three photos with no names attached.
8. No addresses on the menu pages; nothing says which branch is which in the photos; no map links.

## Design reasoning

- **Palette**: deep green `#123f26` and pale gold `#e5da85` are the exact colours of their current site
  theme ("the cozy green cafe"); logo gold `#e3b849`; monogram teal `#004645` for icons. The plates
  supply the only other colours: cream glaze, terracotta, ochre, paprika, cobalt — used only in the
  plate-rim band and price accents, never as blobs. Evening mode darkens the greens and swaps gold for
  the warm brass of the counter pendants.
- **Type**: Inknut Antiqua for headings is the heading font their Squarespace site already uses; their
  body face, Acumin Pro, is Adobe-only, so Archivo (a free grotesque of the same build, with a width
  axis for small caps-style labels) stands in.
- **Ornament**: three things lifted from them, nothing else: the rim of their painted plates (ochre eyes
  on cream between drip lines) as the section divider; the leaf sprig from their monogram; the brass
  dome pendants over the counter, which light up on wine nights.
- **Layout**: no hero. The first phone screen is the two real shopfronts as "doors", each topped by a
  black fascia with gold lettering (Brixton's gold M, Dulwich's "No. 75") and a live open/closed line,
  then the first plates. Food photos are cropped round, on their own plates, because that's how
  every one of their photos is composed.
- **Signature interactions**: (1) the Brixton / Dulwich switch carries through prices (they differ),
  hours, phone, directions, what's on and the booking form, and is remembered; (2) on Friday and
  Saturday from 6pm, with Brixton chosen, the site turns to wine night: darker room, lamps on, wine
  first and brunch "tomorrow from 9am". There's a preview toggle so the owners can see it in daytime.

## What the owners most want this site to do

1. **Send each visitor to the right Madelina while it's open** — two branches with different hours
   (Brixton till 4 at weekends plus evenings, Dulwich till 3), and the current site has no hours and
   phone links that go to Google searches for an old name.
2. **Show the food, on those plates, well enough to bring people in** — the photography is their best
   asset and reviewers name dishes; the current site shows three unlabelled pictures and no drinks.
3. **Turn wine nights, garden tables and private hire into enquiries instead of Instagram DMs** —
   every one of these is currently "DM to book", and weekend booking is "recommended".
